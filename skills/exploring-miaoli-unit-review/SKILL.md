# Exploring Miaoli — Unit Review

The user is Yusuf, a member of the EERC team writing the Exploring Miaoli picture book series. When they share a unit draft, they want the **same analysis and the same document layout every time**. The model answer is Book 1 Unit 3 (Sky Mending Day) in `references/unit3-example.md`. Read it before writing anything and match its structure, tone and level of detail.

Do not invent a different structure, and do not just return a rewritten story. The deliverable is the Page Plan document described below.

## Before you start

1. Read `references/series-guidelines.md`. It has the official targets for each book.
2. Read `references/unit3-example.md` to see the expected output.
3. Work out from the draft which book and unit this is, the festival or place, and the main character. If the book or unit number is unclear, ask.
4. If the user names a reference edit to follow (for example "in the style of Angela's Mazu edit"), use it for the style comparison. If they don't, compare the draft with the house style rules below.

## House style (from the team's edits so far)

Apply these to every revision. They come from Angela's co-edit of the Baishatun Mazu story and were applied in Unit 3.

- **One idea per page.** Split or merge pages so each one carries a single story beat. Move a question to the page where it gets answered if that keeps one idea per page.
- **One short sentence per line**, easy to read aloud.
- **Present simple throughout.** Legends are framed as stories ("In the story, …"), not as facts.
- **One repeated sentence frame**, used about three times so children can join in. Keep the question form the same each time.
- **Join-in sounds and actions** (for example "No, no, no!", "Clap, clap, clap!", "BOOM! BOOM!"). Don't pile up extra sound words.
- **Cut what the picture already shows.** If an illustration can carry a sentence ("He is warm. He is full. He is happy."), cut it and move it to the illustration idea.
- **Hard words are not core vocabulary.** Cut them, or keep them with picture support and say in the Note that a teacher explains them and children don't need to learn them.
- **Fewer place names and repeated questions in the text.** Show the setting in the illustration.
- **Pictures carry the meaning.** Illustration ideas should show clearly who is speaking, use everyday contemporary clothing unless the festival needs otherwise, use ordinary lighting, and leave out unexplained extras.
- **Flag cultural points** on the page where they come up, for review before finalising (religious figures, offerings, fire, incense, ceremony rules, names and spellings).

## Words per page

Let the storyline decide each page's length, guided by the book's target in `references/series-guidelines.md` (Book 1: about 20–40 words; Book 2: about 40–70; Book 3: about 70–120).

- Don't pad a page to reach a number. A short page that lets the picture tell the story is good. Unit 3 pages ran 14–28 words.
- Give each revised page's word count.
- If a page falls well outside the book's range, say why in its Note (for example "short on purpose: the picture carries the moment").
- Report the totals in the style comparison: pages and words before, pages and words after.

## Required story elements (series guidelines)

Check that the story has a setting, characters, a mission, a problem, a solution and a clear ending, and that it includes Miaoli culture, nature, people, history or everyday life (CLIL). If one is missing or weak, say so in the Note for the relevant page or under "For the team".

## Output: the Unit Page Plan document

Produce a Word document (.docx) unless the user asks for something else. Use this exact structure, as in the Unit 3 example:

**Title:** `Exploring Miaoli — Book <N> Unit <N>`
**Subtitle line:** `<Date> UPDATE · <Unit topic> story revised<, in the style of …>`
**Key line:** `Key: black = original draft · purple = suggested edit`

### 1 · Style comparison
- One short paragraph: what the reference style does, the draft's page and word counts, and the revised page and word counts.
- A three-column table: **What the style does** | **<Topic> draft before** | **<Topic> revised**. Use one row per house-style point that applies: short pages, one sentence per line, repeated frame, join-in sounds, hard words, pictures carry meaning, cultural points flagged.

### 2 · Unit information
A table with these rows:
- Unit Topic
- Story Outline (two or three sentences: who, what they want, what goes wrong, how it ends)
- Learning goals → Vocabulary Words (8–10 picturable words for Book 1)
- Learning goals → Sentence Pattern (the repeated frame)
- Learning goals → Cultural Understanding (one sentence a child could repeat)

### 3 · Page Plan
A four-column table: **Page | Story Text | Illustration Ideas | Note**.
- **Story Text:** the original text for that page (labelled with its original spread or page number, in black or grey), then `Revised (<N> words):` followed by the revised text in purple, one sentence per line.
- **Illustration Ideas:** setting, who is in the scene, expressions and actions. Be specific and visual, because these descriptions will guide image generation later.
- **Note:** what changed and why, which pages were merged or split, which words were cut or are supported by pictures, where children join in, and any cultural review flag.

After the table, add one line about the pages reserved for the activity and language-review pages (in Book 1 these are the last two pages of the 12). Activities must work without stickers.

### 4 · For the team (keep it short)
- **Still to confirm:** open decisions, such as mascot name ("Miaoli Meow" in the team story or "Miaoli Cat" in the guidelines), page count, running order or links to the next unit, and which name to use for the festival.
- **Cultural check needed:** each flagged point, with its page number.

Keep the wording plain and concise, in British spelling as in the examples, and keep the same calm, practical tone as Unit 3. Never change the author's story idea or theme. Revise the language and page structure, and suggest rather than impose (that's why edits are shown in purple).

## Formatting the .docx

If a docx skill is available, use it. Put the original text in black or grey and the revised text in purple. Use tables for sections 1–3. Name the file `Exploring_Miaoli_Book<N>_Unit<N>_<Topic>_Page_Plan_<DDMon>.docx`.
